# Influences & References — first draft for review

Draft content for the `Influences` page. **Everything here needs your yes or no.**

Two tags:

- **[yours]** — named or clearly implied in `audax-os-re-frame.md`, `audax-labs.md`, `../ARK/README.md`, or the existing site.
- **[candidate]** — my inference. Cut freely. Publishing an influence you don't actually claim is worse than omitting one, and it writes other people's work into a story they aren't part of.

Every entry carries **what we take** and **where it stops**. The second line is the point. A page that only praises its ancestors is a shrine, and a shrine can't be contributed to. The "where it stops" lines are written to be fair, not clever — if any land as cheap shots, say so and I'll redraft.

*Touches* refers to the map's needs: `context` · `legible-work` · `commitments` · `value` · `relationships` · `learning` · `purpose` · `agent-mandates` · `interop`

---

## 0 · Why this page exists

> Audax OS is not being invented. It is being assembled.
>
> The world does not lack organisational methodologies. It lacks a coherent way to navigate and
> integrate them without becoming a part-time anthropologist of governance. Most of what a
> distributed, fractional, purpose-led organisation needs already exists somewhere — in a
> co-operative's equity model, a Dutch nursing organisation's team structure, a protocol written for
> machines, a 1990 book about irrigation systems.
>
> What is missing is the grammar that lets those pieces be used together.
>
> This page is the working bibliography of that assembly: what we are reading, what we take from it,
> and where each one stops. It is deliberately incomplete. The last section is a request.

## 1 · Organisational form and self-management

### Reinventing Organisations — Frederic Laloux, 2014 **[yours]**
The synthesis that gave "Teal" its currency: self-management, wholeness, evolutionary purpose,
drawn from a dozen organisational case studies.

- **What we take:** the case that self-management is an operating architecture and not a mood, and
  the discipline of describing real organisations rather than ideal ones.
- **Where it stops:** thin on power, conflict and accountability when they get expensive; the case
  studies are overwhelmingly European and North American; and adoption tended to require a
  sympathetic founder, which makes it a gift rather than an infrastructure. Nothing in it
  anticipates a non-human participant.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `commitments` · `relationships`

### Teal for Startups, 2016–2017 **[yours]**
An online volunteer working group applying *Reinventing Organisations* to organisations that were
still forming. Its first named deliverable was a *"Development Map"* for organising the knowledge a
next-generation startup needs, to be released into the public domain.

- **What we take:** the question, and the unfinished deliverable — the map on this site is a second
  attempt at an artifact that working group set out to draw. The commons commitment carries through
  too: public domain then, CC0 in Lab №1 now.
- **Where it stops:** it produced a conversation and a community; it did not produce the map. The
  collaboration framework was not adequate to turn distributed interest into sustained contribution
  — which is itself one of the problems this site now exists to work on.
- **Touches:** all

### Sociocracy and Sociocracy 3.0 — Endenburg; Bockelbrink, Priest & David **[candidate]**
Consent-based decision-making, circles, double-linking; S3 breaks the method into adoptable
patterns rather than a whole system.

- **What we take:** consent as a decision rule that scales without consensus theatre, and — more
  importantly — S3's *pattern* form, which is the closest existing model for how Audax components
  might be adopted one at a time instead of all at once.
- **Where it stops:** built for synchronous circles of people who meet. Says little about fractional
  members, asynchronous decisions, or who consents on behalf of an agent.
- **Touches:** `commitments` · `legible-work`

### Holacracy — Brian Robertson / HolacracyOne **[candidate]**
Roles rather than jobs, explicit accountabilities, governance meetings that change the structure
itself.

- **What we take:** the separation of *role* from *person* — the single most useful move available
  to a fractional organisation, where one person may hold three roles at fifteen percent each.
- **Where it stops:** high ceremony, steep cost of entry, and a constitution that behaves like
  software but is executed by tired humans. Several well-known adopters have since dropped it.
- **Touches:** `legible-work` · `commitments`

### Morning Star's CLOU — Colleague Letter of Understanding **[candidate]**
A tomato processor where each person negotiates a written commitment with the colleagues they
affect, renewed annually, with no managers.

- **What we take:** possibly the best existing answer to *what has this person actually committed
  to* — a peer-negotiated, written, revisable commitment. This is close to what a fractional
  contributor needs on day one.
- **Where it stops:** one company, one industry, largely full-time and co-located, annual cadence.
  A fractional contributor's commitments change faster than a tomato season.
- **Touches:** `commitments` · `value`

### RenDanHeYi — Haier **[candidate]**
A large manufacturer restructured into thousands of self-governing micro-enterprises that contract
with one another and with the market.

- **What we take:** the clearest working evidence that the *organisation family* and *ecosystem*
  layers are real operating layers with their own mechanics, not just a zoom level on a diagram.
- **Where it stops:** internal-market logic can reproduce exactly the extraction Audax is trying to
  move past, and it depends on a scale almost no reader of this site has.
- **Touches:** `interop` · `value`

### The Agile Manifesto, 2001 **[yours]**
Cited in the reframe as the model: a framework that belongs to nobody and became a common grammar.

- **What we take:** the precedent, and the warning. Agile is the proof that an unowned framework can
  reshape an industry — and also the proof of what happens next: certification, consultancies, and
  a word that stopped meaning anything.
- **Where it stops:** it governed how software gets built, never how organisations hold value,
  relationships, learning or purpose. And its ownerlessness left no one able to defend it.
- **Touches:** `legible-work`

## 2 · Human development

### An Everyone Culture — Robert Kegan & Lisa Lahey, 2016 **[yours, by language]**
Deliberately Developmental Organisations: workplaces built so that people's growth is the business
model, not a benefit.

- **What we take:** the phrase itself — the reframe's recommended replacement for
  "purposeful-looking internally" is *deliberately developmental* — and the argument that
  development is structural rather than programmatic.
- **Where it stops:** its exemplars are intense, high-disclosure, full-time cultures that would be
  invasive at best for someone contributing six hours a week to four organisations. The reframe's
  own warning applies squarely here: *developmental can become intrusive*. Consent, privacy and
  limits are load-bearing, and this literature is light on them.
- **Touches:** `learning` · `relationships`

### Communities of Practice — Etienne Wenger **[candidate]**
Learning as participation in a practice, not transmission of content.

- **What we take:** the account of how expertise actually moves — which is exactly the thing
  distributed work removed, and exactly what fractional contributors need when they learn across
  four organisations rather than inside one.
- **Where it stops:** descriptive rather than constructive. Tells you what a healthy community of
  practice looks like; not how to build one from strangers in six time zones.
- **Touches:** `learning` · `context`

## 3 · Value, contribution and ownership

### Slicing Pie — Mike Moyer **[yours, by implication]**
Dynamic equity: contribution accrues in real time at agreed rates, so the split reflects what people
actually put in rather than what they guessed at the start.

- **What we take:** the core move — recognise contribution *as it happens*, before money exists.
  This is the nearest working ancestor of the site's Contribution Ecology Ledger.
- **Where it stops:** it converts everything to a cash-equivalent, which is precisely what the
  reframe says is insufficient. An introduction that changes the organisation's future, or the
  emotional labour of holding a conflict, does not price. Nor does agent-assisted work.
- **Touches:** `value` · `commitments`

### Steward-ownership — Purpose Foundation and others **[candidate]**
Legal structures that separate economic rights from control, so a company cannot be sold out from
under its purpose.

- **What we take:** proof that *purpose-led* can be a legal fact rather than a stated intention —
  and a direct answer to the reframe's demand that purpose become operational rather than ornamental.
- **Where it stops:** it protects the organisation's purpose from its owners. It does nothing about
  how contribution is recognised inside, which is where fractional organisations actually fail.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `value`

### Co-operative and platform-cooperative practice — incl. Trebor Scholz **[yours]**
The reframe names cooperative and dynamic-equity practitioners as people to gather.

- **What we take:** a century and a half of worked answers on membership, contribution, surplus and
  democratic control — the deepest existing body on *what does it mean to belong to this*.
- **Where it stops:** the membership model generally assumes you belong to one, substantially. It
  handles the person who is one-fifth in four places poorly, which is the Audax reader.
- **Touches:** `value` · `commitments`

### DAOs, bounties and token-based contribution **[yours]**
The site's own Who For? page already addresses groups experimenting with tokens, bounties and
dynamic ownership.

- **What we take:** genuinely new instrumentation — programmable contribution accounting, public
  ledgers of who did what, and coordination among people who never meet.
- **Where it stops:** what gets counted gets gamed; the hardest contributions are the least
  countable; and a decade in, governance participation is thin and captured. The reframe's warning
  is exact — value accounting must not become a metrics regime with better cryptography.
- **Touches:** `value` · `legible-work`

## 4 · Commons and governance

### Governing the Commons — Elinor Ostrom, 1990 **[candidate]**
Eight design principles for shared resources governed neither by market nor state, drawn from
irrigation systems, fisheries and forests that lasted centuries.

- **What we take:** the strongest available answer to the reframe's own hardest open question —
  *"open ownership versus accountable stewardship."* Ostrom's principles (clear boundaries,
  proportional cost and benefit, collective choice, graduated sanctions, nested enterprises) are a
  ready-made charter skeleton for Audax Labs. **Nested enterprises** in particular maps directly
  onto the five layers.
- **Where it stops:** written for rivalrous physical resources among people who share a place.
  Knowledge commons behave differently, and a global, transient, partly-agentic membership breaks
  several of the principles as written.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `commitments` · `interop`

### Open source governance and licensing — incl. CC0 **[yours]**
ARK is released CC0, a public domain dedication rather than a licence.

- **What we take:** the decision is already made and is a real commitment: no copyright asserted, no
  attribution required, contributions dedicated to the public domain.
- **Where it stops:** CC0 answers the code question and leaves everything else open — data, models,
  participant material, community knowledge, names and marks, and whether "Audax OS" can be claimed
  by an implementation that violates its principles. `audax-labs.md` lists this as Priority 4 and it
  is unresolved. It is a blank on the map.
- **Touches:** `interop`

### The internet's own architecture — TCP/IP, HTTP, and the IETF **[yours]**
Named in the reframe as reference-architecture analogies.

- **What we take:** the shape of the ambition — specify the interfaces, let a thousand
  implementations differ, and let rough consensus plus running code decide. This is the honest
  meaning of "reference architecture" and the answer to the framework-versus-product tension.
- **Where it stops:** protocols coordinate machines that do not have interests. Organisational
  standards coordinate people who do — power, law, money and identity are in the room, and no
  amount of specification makes them interoperate.
- **Touches:** `interop` · `agent-mandates`

## 5 · Distributed and explicit practice

### The GitLab Handbook **[candidate]**
Around two thousand pages of a company's operating system, written down, public, and edited by
anyone who works there.

- **What we take:** the most complete existing demonstration that the invisible office can be made
  explicit — and that *handbook-first* is a discipline, not a document. It is the closest thing
  anyone has built to *context that travels*.
- **Where it stops:** it is one company's context, not a portable grammar, and it is famously
  unreadable at volume. Two thousand pages is not context that travels; it is context that has been
  stored. And no agent was in the room when it was designed.
- **Touches:** `context` · `legible-work`

### Loomio, Cobudget and the Enspiral network **[candidate]**
A New Zealand-rooted network of people and ventures that has run distributed, fractional,
purpose-led collaboration in practice for over a decade, and built its own tooling for decisions
and money.

- **What we take:** possibly the closest existing thing to the Audax reader — actually distributed,
  actually fractional, actually purpose-led, with real instruments for collective decisions and
  budgets, and unusually honest public writing about what went wrong.
- **Where it stops:** it stayed at the scale of a network of ventures and never became a
  transferable architecture; much of what makes it work is relational and does not port. Predates
  agentic participation entirely.
- **Touches:** `commitments` · `value` · `relationships`

> This one I would particularly like your view on. If Enspiral is a real influence it is one of the
> strongest entries available; if it is not, it should not be here at all.

## 6 · Agentic systems and protocols

### Model Context Protocol **[candidate]**
An open protocol for connecting agents to tools, data and context, with a growing multi-vendor
implementation base.

- **What we take:** the working example of the interop layer the reframe asks for — *tools that
  interoperate rather than capture* — and a live demonstration that an open protocol can beat
  platform lock-in in this domain.
- **Where it stops:** it standardises the plumbing between an agent and a resource. It says nothing
  about mandate, permission, escalation, attribution or accountability — the organisational
  questions Audax is actually asking.
- **Touches:** `interop` · `agent-mandates`

### Radicle **[yours, via ARK]**
Peer-to-peer, sovereign code collaboration without a central forge.

- **What we take:** an existing answer to sovereignty in shared infrastructure — organisations
  collaborating without a host who can evict them. Named as a research thread in ARK.
- **Where it stops:** solves hosting and identity for code. The harder Audax question is governed
  shared *memory* across organisational boundaries, where the substrate is conversation rather than
  commits.
- **Touches:** `interop` · `context`

### Decentralised identifiers and verifiable credentials **[candidate]**
Standards for identity and claims that a person holds and presents, rather than a platform issuing.

- **What we take:** the mechanism the reframe asks for by name — *portable reputation and learning
  records*, and *cross-organisational identity* — for someone contributing to four organisations who
  should not have to rebuild their standing in each.
- **Where it stops:** a decade of standards with very thin adoption, and a credential says what
  someone was certified for, not what they actually contributed or how well they held a hard
  conversation.
- **Touches:** `learning` · `value` · `relationships`

### Transcript-native intelligence — the Living Book thread **[yours, via ARK]**
Conversations as the source of truth, with documents as artefacts rather than the record.

- **What we take:** it is the working hypothesis of Lab №1 — that institutional memory should be
  built from what was actually said, attributed and inspectable, rather than reconstructed from what
  got written up afterwards.
- **Where it stops:** unproven, and it makes the surveillance risk acute. `audax-labs.md` puts it
  plainly: without clear memory categories and permissions, the hive mind becomes a surveillance
  beehive.
- **Touches:** `context` · `agent-mandates` · `learning`

## 7 · Purpose, regeneration and economics

### Regenerative practice and the RegenWorld network **[yours]**
The Gathering, Camp Navarro, and the wider regenerative field in which Camp Audax sits.

- **What we take:** the living context and the people. This is where Audax's outward purpose is
  already being practised rather than argued.
- **Where it stops:** rich in intention and gathering, thinner in operating infrastructure — which
  is precisely the gap Audax proposes to work in, and the reason a camp is a lab and not a
  conference.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `relationships`

### Doughnut Economics — Kate Raworth **[candidate]**
Value bounded below by a social floor and above by an ecological ceiling.

- **What we take:** a defensible frame for *"value delivery measured by more than financial means"* —
  the reframe's own line — without reaching for language the reframe warns is too culturally coded.
- **Where it stops:** operates at the scale of economies and cities. Turning it into a
  fifteen-person distributed organisation's value accounting is unsolved, and mostly attempted
  through reporting frameworks that consume more effort than they return.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `value`

### Post-extractivist and commons-transition thinking **[yours, with a caveat]**
Named in the reframe, and simultaneously flagged there as too culturally coded to lead with.

- **What we take:** the root commitment — that people, communities and the living world are not
  inputs to financial extraction.
- **Where it stops:** as public positioning it selects for people who already agree. The reframe's
  own instruction is to translate it into concrete organisational behaviour, which is what this
  entire site is trying to do. It belongs on this page, not on the front one.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `value`

## 8 · Systems and pattern thinking

### The Viable System Model — Stafford Beer **[candidate]**
A recursive model of what any viable organisation must contain, where every viable system holds
viable systems and is held by one.

- **What we take:** it is the nearest ancestor of the five layers. *Recursion* — the same functions
  appearing at individual, team, organisation, family and ecosystem scale — is Beer's central claim,
  and it gives the layers a rigorous defence they currently lack.
- **Where it stops:** cybernetic and control-theoretic in vocabulary, genuinely hard to read, and
  its historical association with Project Cybersyn cuts both ways. It also assumes a viable system
  has a boundary, which is exactly what fractional participation dissolves.
- **Touches:** all — this is a candidate for the map's structure, not one region

### A Pattern Language — Christopher Alexander **[candidate]**
Solutions to recurring problems, each stated with its context and its forces, each linking to
larger and smaller patterns.

- **What we take:** the form. The reframe asks for *protocols and patterns* as a distinct layer
  between principles and implementations, and this is where that idea comes from — including the
  discipline that a pattern must name the conditions under which it does not apply.
- **Where it stops:** Alexander himself concluded the pattern language had largely failed at what he
  wanted it to do. Patterns transmit solutions well and the judgement to choose between them
  poorly — which is the reframe's own "framework may be too conceptually large" risk, arriving early.
- **Touches:** all

### Thinking in Systems — Donella Meadows **[candidate]**
Stocks, flows, feedback, and where to intervene in a system.

- **What we take:** the leverage-point discipline — most of the reframe's "smallest useful
  implementation" question is really *which single intervention is worth its cost*.
- **Where it stops:** a way of seeing rather than a way of building. It will not tell you what to
  put in a contributor agreement.
- **Touches:** `purpose` · `learning`

## 9 · What we haven't read yet

> This list is incomplete, and visibly so.
>
> We are missing whole traditions — most of what has been worked out about organising outside
> Europe and North America, most of what unions and mutual-aid movements know about commitment and
> obligation, most of what indigenous governance knows about holding a commons across generations,
> and almost certainly a working answer to a question we have listed as blank.
>
> If something belongs here, tell us. Say what it gives us, and say where it stops — the second part
> is the part we need.
